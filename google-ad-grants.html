Google Ad Grants: How Your 501(c)(3) Can Get $10,000 a Month in Free Advertising

Updated 2026 · 501c3 Made Simple

Most nonprofit founders assume advertising is something you either pay for or skip entirely. Google's Ad Grants program is the exception — and it's one of the least-used benefits of having 501(c)(3) status. Eligible organizations receive up to $10,000 every month in free search advertising on Google, with no catch and no expiration on the benefit as long as your account stays active and compliant.

What the Google Ad Grants Program Actually Is

Google Ad Grants is part of Google for Nonprofits, Google's broader initiative offering free and discounted tools to registered charities. The advertising grant specifically gives your organization $10,000 per month in in-kind Google Search ad credit — not cash, but real ad spend that shows your website at the top of relevant search results when people search for terms related to your mission.

Unlike traditional Google Ads, this isn't a trial or a limited-time promotion. Nonprofits have held active grants for years, some since the program's earlier iterations. The main requirement is ongoing compliance with Google's policies — more on that below.

Not Eligible

Government entities, hospitals and healthcare organizations (in most cases), and schools/universities typically do not qualify for Ad Grants specifically, even though many can access other Google for Nonprofits tools.

What You Can (and Can't) Do With the Grant

The $10,000/month applies specifically to Google Search text ads — the ads that show above organic search results. It does not cover display ads, YouTube ads, or Google Shopping. There are also account structure rules unique to Ad Grants: campaigns must maintain a minimum click-through rate, use at least two ad groups per campaign, and follow specific bidding restrictions (Ad Grants accounts historically operated on a capped manual CPC, though Google has shifted some accounts toward Maximize Conversions bidding — check your account's current settings).

Accounts that don't meet these ongoing performance and structure requirements can be suspended, so this isn't a "set it and forget it" benefit — it needs light, regular maintenance.

How to Apply

  1. Register for Google for Nonprofits at google.com/nonprofits and verify your organization (Google typically partners with a verification service like TechSoup or Percent to confirm your 501(c)(3) status).
  2. Apply for the Ad Grant from within your Google for Nonprofits dashboard once your account is verified.
  3. Build a compliant website if you don't already have one — Google reviews site quality as part of approval.
  4. Set up your first campaigns following Ad Grants-specific structure requirements (these differ from a standard paid Google Ads account).

Why This Matters More Than Founders Realize

$10,000 a month is $120,000 a year in advertising — more than many small nonprofits' entire annual budget. Used well, it can drive volunteer sign-ups, donor traffic, and awareness for programs that would otherwise rely entirely on word of mouth or social media reach. The catch isn't cost; it's that most nonprofit founders simply don't know the program exists, or assume the application process is more complicated than it is.
