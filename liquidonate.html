LiquiDonate: $100 Million in Free Products for Nonprofits

Updated 2026 · 501c3 Made Simple

Retailers process an enormous volume of returned, overstock, and liquidated inventory every year — far more than most people realize. LiquiDonate was built to intercept a portion of that inventory before it's discarded and route it directly to nonprofits, at no cost.

What LiquiDonate Is

LiquiDonate is a donation-matching platform that connects retailers with excess inventory to verified nonprofit organizations. Since launching, the platform has facilitated more than $100 million in product donations — everything from furniture and electronics to clothing and office supplies — delivered directly to nonprofits that request them.

The model is straightforward: retailers list available surplus inventory, nonprofits browse and request what they need, and LiquiDonate coordinates delivery — typically faster than older donation-matching models, since the platform emphasizes speed and local/regional matching where possible.

What's Typically Available

  • Furniture (office and household)
  • Electronics
  • Clothing
  • General office and organizational supplies

How to Request Donations

  1. Create a nonprofit account at LiquiDonate.com.
  2. Complete verification with your organization's 501(c)(3) documentation.
  3. Browse currently available inventory in your area or category of need.
  4. Submit a request — approved requests are typically coordinated for delivery directly to your organization.

Practical Tip

Because LiquiDonate emphasizes fast, local matching, availability can be regional. A nonprofit in one area may see different inventory than one across the country — check regularly rather than assuming a single visit reflects everything on the platform.

Why It's Worth Setting Up

For nonprofits running direct-service programs — shelters, community closets, disaster response, back-to-school drives — free access to real inventory can meaningfully reduce program costs. Since this is ongoing (not a one-time drive), it's worth building into your regular resource-sourcing routine rather than treating it as a single opportunity.
